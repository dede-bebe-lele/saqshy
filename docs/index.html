    04 · how it works

    Satellites + weather + gradient-boosted trees

    01

    Split the country

    Kazakhstan is cut into 1,326 squares of half a degree (about 55 × 37 km).

    02

    Read the evening

    For each square: NASA VIIRS fire detections from the last day, week, month and year, fires in neighbouring squares, and NASA POWER weather: heat, humidity, wind, rain, soil dryness. From these we also compute the two classic fire-danger indices (FWI and Nesterov).

    03

    Rank for tomorrow

    A gradient-boosted tree model (scikit-learn) gives each square a probability of fire tomorrow. The top 10% become the red zone and the alert.

    What the model relies on most

    Honest test design

    • Only information available on the evening before is used. The model never sees tomorrow's weather.
    • Train on 2012–2022, test on 2023–2025: no test day was seen in training.
    • "Usual for this month" uses only earlier years.
    • Baselines: recent fires, the cell's usual fire rate, the Nesterov index (official in Russia), and the Canadian FWI used by Europe's fire service EFFIS.
    05 · who it's for

    From a ranger's evening to a business

    Rangers · МЧС

    Rangers and the Emergency Ministry

    Send patrols, drones and fire crews to the red squares first, and pre-position equipment the evening before.

    Farmers

    Farmers and herders

    A Kazakh or Russian alert: don't burn stubble tomorrow, move hay and animals, plough a firebreak.

    Insurers

    Insurers and forestry

    Crop insurance in Kazakhstan is voluntary and the state pays half the premium, so insurers need risk maps. Wildfire risk is a real market: OroraTech raised €37M, Pano AI $44M, Overstory $43M.
